## 6. What was investigated, what was changed, what was not (narrative; numbers are in the tables above and in the CSV / JSON files)

### 6.1 Hypothesis
"Instruction information becomes weak or lost during sequential decoding, arm-mode switching, later stages and recovery, and this limits Randomized success."

Outcome in one paragraph: the instruction was never *absent* (the executor re-encodes the episode instruction with every refreshed observation), but in the previous frozen model it was *used* only by the first-stage referent scorer of click_bell and stack_blocks_two. One concrete loss of instruction information was found and repaired (second-stage target selection and stage verification of stack_blocks_two). That repair makes the model follow the role-exchanged stack instruction end to end, which the previous model could not do at all. It does not raise native Randomized success: the measured Randomized failures of the four tasks are grounding-precision and reachability failures, not language failures.

### 6.2 Trace of the instruction through the rollout (source + executed)
1. `v21_eval.py` draws one RoboTwin instruction per episode and passes the string to `policy.run`. The executor keeps the string for the whole episode.
2. Every VLM pass of the rollout (`tokens()`: episode start, every observation view, StagePlan call, every decoder call, every progress check, every retry) tokenises this string again together with the current three images. Nothing in `acl_rt_policy.py` / `pic_rt_policy.py` replaces it by a constant; the single place that looked suspicious (`bim_rt_policy.place_target`: `self.tokens(imgs, "", ...) if False else ...`) is dead code and uses the episode instruction.
3. Consumers of the resulting token states: (a) referent scorer: language rows only; (b) place-referent scorer: language rows; (c) StagePlan head, AdaptiveDecoder (both slots = all arm modes), ProgressHead / arm-mode head: all prefix tokens (image + language + state) through attention; no detach, no overwrite.
4. Task-specific constants that carry task semantics instead of language: arm selection (`pick_arm`, a geometric classifier), the referent height prior, the const / memory-relative place targets, the stage count. Checkpoints are task-specific, so within a task the instruction only varies in wording except for stack_blocks_two (block roles) and the two-bell click_bell scenes.

### 6.3 Interventions (development seeds only)
Same scenes, instruction replaced at every VLM pass (`PIC_LANG`); table in section 2.
* handover_block, place_container_plate: blank / shuffled / wrong-task instruction change native success by at most a few episodes of n = 12-20, i.e. not distinguishable from the run-to-run variation measured on identical seeds (place_container_plate, same seeds, same model: 3, 5 and 6 of 12). These tasks have no semantically valid alternative instruction; their models are not language-dependent and no counterfactual supervision exists to make them so. This is a limitation, not a success.
* click_bell: blank 11/20 and wrong-task 13/20 against 15-16/20 with the original or word-shuffled instruction: a small language effect of the counterfactually trained scorer (single-bell native scenes; the two-bell counterfactual is scorer-level only, see `LANGUAGE_PATH_VERIFICATION.md`).
* stack_blocks_two, previous model: the role-exchanged instruction switches the FIRST grasp to the green block in 10 of 12 episodes (original: red in 19 of 20), but the exchanged goal was reached in 0 of 12: the second target was never selected. Cause: the cycle scorer adds the stage index to the pooled instruction vector (logit = f(instruction) + g(stage)), so the stage cannot change which words are read; at stage 1 it selects "green" whatever the instruction says (on-policy frames: 0.00 accuracy when the instructed second block is red).
* Per-call probes (`PIC_PROBE`, same observation / state / grounded target, other instruction; n = 24-51 decoder calls, 8-22 StagePlan calls per task): the decoder chunk moves by 0.1 cm (counterfactual) to 1.0 cm (blank) on average, gripper commands flip in 0-8 % of calls, the arm mode never flips. StagePlan grasp xyz moves by 0.2 cm under the counterfactual instruction and 2-5 cm under a blank one. The decoder and StagePlan therefore receive the instruction's content through the grounded target / place / stage conditioning, not through the language tokens. With the new progress head the stage verdict flips in 73 % of the calls under the counterfactual instruction (handover head: 0-4 %).

### 6.4 What was implemented and trained
* `pic_common.SubgoalScorer`: the original HR scorer with a stage-specific offset of the learned text query (one new tensor, zero-initialised). Trained with the original recipe on original + randomized frames + paired counterfactual scenes + 655 on-policy subgoal samples from training-domain rollouts under both instructions (labels: privileged projections, training only).
* `pic_common.PicProgressHead`: ProgressHead + explicit instruction pathway (attention pooling of the instruction-token states -> residual logits). Only the pathway is trained (the loaded head and the decoder stay bit-identical). Supervision: every progress-check observation encoded with both valid instructions, outcome judged against each instruction (wrong referent held / placed = FAILED_OR_RECOVERY). A first version that trained the whole head memorised the training episodes (held-out accuracy on the alternative instruction 0.24); it was discarded.
* Persistent instruction state: the instruction-token states of the first VLM pass are cached and fed to the instruction pathway at every later stage, arm mode and retry (held-out accuracy with the cached state equals the live one).
* Executor (`pic_rt_policy.py`, a versioned copy; the frozen `acl_rt_policy.py` is untouched): rejected-referent memory, instruction interventions, probes, data recorders.
* Not trained: decoder, StagePlan and arm-mode instruction conditioning. No counterfactual action supervision exists (the expert demonstrations contain one block order), and the measurements above show that the cf goal is executed correctly by the unchanged decoder once the subgoal is selected correctly. Adding an untrained-in-effect language term to the decoder would have changed weights without a testable benefit.

### 6.5 Remaining Randomized bottlenecks: what the data show
* place_container_plate: the "55 % -> 40 %" difference between the two earlier finals is not an executor regression. On identical development seeds (n = 40) the previous-study executor scored 14 and the current executor 12; refreshed proposal heads (3x randomized views), reachability-aware view selection and both together scored 15, 14 and 16. None of these differences exceeds the noise. Failure mechanism (final2 logs): two-view grounding succeeds in only 3 of 20 Randomized episodes (wrist proposals missing; half of the commanded observation poses are not reached), the StagePlan regression fallback then misses the container rim. Not solved.
* handover_block: both grasps of the episode succeed only when the horizontal error is below about 1.2 cm (training-domain rollouts: first grasp 17/19 vs 4/13; receive 18/20 vs 0/7). The triangulated point has a median error of 1.1 cm. Tested and rejected on training-domain seeds: gripper-yaw misalignment as the cause (grasps hold at 20-25 degrees), StagePlan rotation refresh at the observation view (worse: 7.2 vs 6.0 degrees), 3-D re-grounding of the receive point (4.6 vs 2.2 cm), top-down wrist refinement (wrist rays are 30-90 degrees from vertical for this grasp), a retrained AnchorHead (first-grasp successes 18/32 and 14/32 vs 16/32). Not solved; a missed grasp still topples the box and the retries fail.
* stack_blocks_two: reachability-aware views 13/20 vs 15/20 (not adopted). Sequential performance preserved (section 1).
* click_bell: unchanged.

### 6.6 Limitations
* n = 20 per cell; differences of 1-3 episodes are noise. Three of the four tasks are unchanged models, so the average cannot be expected to move, and the aspirational 80 % Randomized average was not reached.
* Causal language dependence is demonstrated for stack_blocks_two (first target, manipulation order, second target, stage verification, final relation) and, at scorer level only, for click_bell. For handover_block and place_container_plate no valid counterfactual instruction exists; nothing is claimed.
* Stage-1 target selection of stack is not shown to depend on language (blank-text accuracy equals valid-text accuracy on held-out frames): once the first block is placed, the scene and the memory gate determine the remaining block.
* Arm roles named in an instruction are not followed (arm choice is geometric); the role-exchanged instructions keep the original arm words.
* The exchanged goal is judged by privileged final block poses (analysis only); the environment has no success flag for it.
* Round-1 subgoal recordings under the exchanged instruction stored the two text-state tensors under each other's name (recorder bug, fixed; corrected in the builder; the affected alternative progress tokens of round 1 are not used).
* The GPUs were idle between 08:56 and 14:50 UTC (session interruption); no job was lost.
